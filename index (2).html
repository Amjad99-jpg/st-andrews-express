Project 01 · Process analysis

Every delivery checked.
Every gap closed.

For three years I ran stock and supplier control for a busy dessert shop in Liverpool: planning orders around student term times and summer, and checking every delivery against the invoice, the till and the stock records.

01 · The job

Two numbers had to be right: what we ordered and what we paid

A small shop feels every mistake in stock. Three things could go wrong at any delivery, and my job was to stop all three.

Over orderCash on the shelf

Order more than the season needs and money sits in stock instead of the business.

Under orderLost sales

Run out of best sellers in a busy week and customers walk away.

Short deliveryPaying for nothing

Pay the invoice without checking and you pay for items that never arrived. Short or missing items were the most common mismatch.

03 · Execution

How I did it, in three moves

01

Planned by season

Read the order history to see how demand moved with student term times and summer, and ordered to that pattern instead of a flat amount.

02

Checked every line

Matched each delivery and invoice against point of sale, inventory and CRM records: item, quantity and price.

03

Closed gaps at source

Traced each mismatch to its cause and settled it directly with the supplier, then tracked ordering, stock and pricing in the team KPI tracker.

05 · Process audit

What I'd change next

Looking back at the process as a business analyst: how to keep the same accuracy with less manual work. These are proposals, not changes I made at the time.

✕How it ran
  1. Every line checked manually against the invoice
  2. Each mismatch chased one at a time
  3. Seasonal plan built from order history

Accurate, but manual.

✓Proposed redesign
  1. A matching sheet that compares order, delivery note and invoice and flags any gap automatically
  2. A supplier scorecard showing who short delivers most often, to raise at supplier reviews
  3. Reorder levels per product that rise ahead of term time and summer

Same accuracy, less time.
